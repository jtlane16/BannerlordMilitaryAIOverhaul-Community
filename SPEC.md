# Bannerlord Military AI Overhaul — campaign commander specification

**Rule 0 — Simple is better.** Use the simplest implementation that meets the agreed behavior. Add complexity only when a concrete requirement or an observed campaign failure shows the simpler approach is insufficient.

**Rule 1 — Log the full lifecycle at every stage.** Write all mod logging to a file on the current user's Desktop, starting with Stage 0 and continuing through every stage. The campaign log must explain what the commander knew, what it decided, what it ordered, and what actually happened. A stage is not complete if its behavior works but cannot be diagnosed from its log.

Complete the spec before writing code. Then build and test **one stage at a time**. This document defines campaign behavior. [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) defines the source layout and the ownership of that behavior. Stage numbers belong to the delivery plan, not to production filenames or types.

## Purpose

Each kingdom has one campaign commander responsible for **objectives across the whole kingdom**, including in the player's kingdom. Several objectives can run at once. An army is a temporary force assigned to one objective at a time; an objective can have several armies. The commander can also raise one army large enough to do the job alone. Its goal is to win wars, not to maximize army size or keep armies permanently in the field.

Every major order should answer three questions: **Why this objective? Why this army? Why now?** Judge a decision by what the commander could know when it made it. It cannot read enemy commanders' private objectives or orders.

## Command authority

| Situation | Who decides |
| --- | --- |
| Independent AI party | Vanilla chooses its orders. When a lord considers defending a besieged town or castle of its own kingdom, being outnumbered no longer discounts that defense. Distance, supplies, and the penalty for sending more defenders to an already well-covered siege still apply. Considering a party for a summons does not redirect it. |
| Forming an AI army | The commander decides whether to create it, who leads, which parties to summon, where to gather, and when to depart or cancel. |
| Summoned party | The commander owns the summons and can cancel it. Bannerlord handles travel to the leader and attachment on arrival. A party on the way is committed but not yet present. |
| Assembled AI army | The commander chooses its objective and directs it alone or alongside other armies. It decides when to divert, withdraw, release members, boost cohesion, spend army-related influence, or disband. |
| Released party | Returns immediately to vanilla party AI. Disbanding an army releases its parties; it does not disband the individual parties. |
| Player-led army | The player retains normal army creation and management. The commander cannot spend influence on its behalf, redirect it, or take its members. The player's main party is not an assignable force. |

**Player-clan AI parties:** The vanilla **Allow joining other armies** toggle (`Hero.CanJoinArmy`) is a hard veto. When off, the commander cannot call the party, reserve it, count its troops toward a proposed force, or appoint it leader to bypass the setting. Check eligibility again when issuing the summons.

**Decision versus execution:** The commander chooses the army's target and issues the native `DefendSettlement` or `BesiegeSettlement` order. Preserve vanilla's short-term execution of that order, including waiting, engaging, preparing camp, and assaulting; Stage 0 must not disable those behaviors when suppressing vanilla target selection. The commander owns voluntary disbandment. If cohesion runs out, the army is destroyed, or another natural cause breaks it up, let the game disperse it.

## Objectives and priority

1. **Defend the kingdom's own settlements.**
2. **Defend allied settlements.**
3. **Besiege enemy settlements.**

This is an order of priority, not a scoring bonus. An active, eligible siege stays on the commander's list until it actually ends or the settlement no longer qualifies for defense. The 72-hour army route limit rejects a response, not the objective. If no force can engage yet, keep looking as parties become available, allies arrive, or besiegers weaken. A defense already adequately covered does not claim every other force. Independent parties can still respond to raids under vanilla AI.

**Defense only while under siege:** Any native siege of an owned town or castle bars that kingdom from starting a new commander offensive muster or march, even when relief is already assigned or cannot get there. Recall existing offensive musters, marches, and camps when native and player locks allow. Reuse the army intact for reachable, uncovered relief; a recalled army without a useful relief job disperses instead of entering idle grace. Do not force participants out of battles, override external orders, or remove unrelated parties from a shared camp. A voluntary recall is not a repulse. The ban lifts when the last home siege ends. An allied siege or village raid alone does not trigger it.

When same-kingdom defenses compete, first preserve adequate cover already at the camp. Within each priority tier (home before allied), reserve armies for the uncovered camp they can reach with **enough combined strength soonest**, using navigable route times and known local support. The last needed arrival sets that ETA. Send any remaining armies to the nearest uncovered camp, even if they are understrength. Town over castle and higher settlement value break ETA ties and prioritize remaining muster work. Count each army once. If a spare can replace a responder and free the only route to a second siege, cover both; do not pull a defender off station in anticipation of a distant replacement. Reconsider current and saved assignments on ordinary planning passes, including armies waiting understrength when a viable alternative appears. An unrelated battle, a cleared order or a fleeing defender is not ready cover. An intact defend order with a tactical `Hold` may be valid local waiting. Physical coverage requires a short navigable path to the camp, not just proximity across water.

If a new plan adequately covers the **same sieges**, keep current viable orders unless the same replacement saves more than both **one campaign hour and 15%** on at least one adequate response, delays no other adequate response, and retains that lead on a later planning pass at least one campaign hour apart. A lost lead or different proposed assignment resets confirmation; a reload starts confirmation fresh. Do not trade away the ability to cover more sieges for a faster ETA. Actual changes in viable coverage do not need ETA confirmation.

Allied relief requires **both** a formal alliance with the settlement's owner and an existing war with the besieger. A common enemy alone does not qualify a settlement. If either condition stops being true, close the allied-relief assignment. Military orders do not initiate a war.

Independent AI lords also consider besieged allied towns and castles and actively raided allied villages, using normal native defense scores. Both kingdoms must be at war with the attacker. While any home fortification is under attack, no allied defense candidates are added. These are solo party options, not army summons or orders.

**Defensive war stance:** When the commander chooses a new siege target, exclude towns and castles of any kingdom the attacker has set to Defensive. Read the player's native war strategy for a player-ruled kingdom or CAI's directional posture for an AI kingdom when available. Recheck before committing a proposal. Changing stance does not cancel an assigned muster, march or camp; it does not restrict the opponent or other wars.

### Native strength and distance references

- **Starting an offensive siege:** Use the game's new-siege strength check for the proposed attacking force. In the default model, a target not already under siege normally scores zero if attacker strength is below **2 × adjusted defense**. The first check includes the garrison, militia, eligible parties inside, a wall bonus reduced by low food, and an estimate of support from the defending kingdom. Nearby enemy parties can affect the subsequent score. This is combat strength, not headcount. It is a check for **starting a siege**, not for assaulting the walls once a camp exists. Use the strength check without giving vanilla the choice of objective.
- **Relieving a town or castle:** The default defense score compares responding strength to the **besiegers**, not the garrison. It discounts a response below `1.1 × max(100, estimated enemy strength)`; that is not a hard rejection. Near the settlement, the normal engagement check wants locally available strength **greater than 1.2 ×** the besiegers on the relevant side; joining an ally already fighting is a separate case. A second army that cannot arrive in time does not help meet a relief requirement.
- **Distance after an objective:** No universal follow-up radius was found. Finishing an army objective clears its target. For new siege targets, vanilla scores the target's first and second rings of neighboring fortifications (the second ring carries one-quarter the weight) and favors shorter navigable routes in its target score. For defense of an actively besieged fortification, vanilla compares estimated travel time against **8.02 days**. This is an outer travel screen, not a prediction of when that particular settlement will fall and not an offensive follow-up radius.

Source: `BannerlordDecompiled/TaleWorlds.CampaignSystem.cs`, `DefaultTargetScoreCalculatingModel.GetTargetScoreForFaction` (320891–321052), `GetNearbyPartyDataWhileDefendingSettlement` (540413–540527), `AiMilitaryBehavior.CalculateDistanceScoreForBesieging` / `GetDistanceScoreForDefending` (42919–43072), and `Army.FinishArmyObjective` (20968–20972).

**Commander relief rule — send what can respond.** Prefer enough combined strength to fight, but **no strength ratio is required to form or dispatch relief**. Use existing armies or muster the best eligible force available near the objective, within the influence budget and higher-priority commitments. If 400 is all that can respond to 600 besiegers, send the 400 with `DefendSettlement`. An army on the way counts as committed, not present; an understrength response does not fully cover the objective. If no eligible army can be formed or redirected, keep the objective open and leave independent lords under vanilla orders.

Give the defend order at departure, not at a waypoint outside the camp. On a normal land relief, vanilla approaches the besiegers, finds a local waiting position or groups with responding parties, and decides whether to engage as the local balance changes. For home and allied army relief, require a navigable ETA of at most **72 campaign hours** to the besieger camp, including any necessary gathering. Do not pay for or project strength from escorts that cannot contribute within the limit. Recheck remaining travel on reviews and reload. An over-limit formation redirects intact if it has another reachable relief job; otherwise disband once safe and return its lords to native AI. No forced battle orders, and no expiry based on the age of the siege. If the settlement actually falls, close relief; retaking it is a separate offensive decision.

If a departed relief army's native defend order was cleared but its army objective still names that siege, fleeing from that same besieger camp alone does not prevent restoring the original order. The usual route, ETA and reissue limits still apply. Other threats, native battle and movement locks still block recovery. This exception does not authorize release or upkeep. A successful restore keeps its assignment for the rest of that planning pass; ordinary reassignment eligibility resumes on the next pass.

**Commander siege order:** Issue `BesiegeSettlement` at departure. The native settlement encounter starts or joins the camp on arrival; preparation and the native assault-readiness decision govern when the camp leader switches to `AssaultSettlement`. Do not create a camp from afar or schedule a separate assault order. Source: `TaleWorlds.CampaignSystem.cs`, `StartSettlementEncounter` (280918–280940), `GetBesiegeBehavior` (539991–540010), `BesiegerCamp.IsReadyToBesiege` (599259–599273), and `GetDefendSettlementBehavior` (540242–540375).

**Selecting an offensive target:** An enemy town or castle must be at most **two links on the native land fortification graph** from a town or castle currently owned by the attacking kingdom. The intervening fief may belong to anyone. An army's movement, an allied holding, or a former holding does not extend the frontier. After covering defenses, choose the nearest feasible target within that frontier by navigable route from the army (or the proposed muster); break equal route distances by higher native settlement value. If none qualifies, do not reach deeper. Recheck before committing summons or orders and on pre-camp review, including after reload. If the last foothold is lost, close the out-of-range assignment when safe and seek reuse normally; this geographic closure is not a paid failure. An established camp is not lifted for geography. The frontier applies to offense, not relief.

**Fresh offensive musters:** First look for a viable existing formation. Before raising a new one, require the native siege check to pass with 90% of the projected roster strength (about **2.22× adjusted defense** under the native default). Select the smallest affordable arrival-order prefix that passes, and recheck before spending.

**Keeping a formation:** Once formed, use the **live roster** at gather, departure and march: every eligible actual member once, irrespective of charge, attachment, or ETA. Refused calls and parties that have left do not count. Use that roster for the 1.6× planning rule (125% probe against the native 2× gate); keep the actual escort orders when departing for offense or relief. Physical strength is distinct: nearby field danger and strength available for a battle use only troops present. If a formation is exposed but can recover safely at its muster with approaching escorts, regroup without a new army or summons. Do not leave that regroup prematurely merely because the enemy is no longer in sight. Failure to progress still ends a stranded assignment. Never rescore an established camp as a new siege.

**Ending a siege assignment is not a disband instruction.** Confirm a strength failure on a later review before ending the job. On closure, give an intact eligible formation first consideration for an uncovered reachable defense and then another viable offensive objective. No new summons, duplicate claim, or repeated objective within an uninterrupted reassignment chain (including reload). Successful captures keep their one-ring immediate follow-on rule. The purchase guards below still constrain replacement spending.

**One day without an assignment:** When a healthy commander army finishes or loses its siege or relief assignment and has no immediate successor job, clear the old order and keep the roster in place for 24 campaign hours. Native `Hold` during gathering, combat recovery or a siege is not an idle transition. Ordinary planners can reuse the army during the day; an actual assignment clears the fixed deadline. No idle upkeep, additional summons or waypoint. Persist the deadline across reload. At expiry, check once more for uncovered reachable relief and then a viable offensive job for this intact formation; otherwise release when native/player locks allow. Stale failed targets do not automatically reopen. Native dispersal, loss of authority and an unsustainable formation retain their own handling.

**Recovering a cleared siege order:** Let native battle and raid cleanup finish. If the same assignment is still valid, restore the same muster or siege order without new summons. Allow at most two reissues without progress. A confirmed restored order earns a fresh budget only after the leader physically moves and improves its best navigable distance to the same waypoint by at least one map unit. Moving back and forth, a shorter distance estimate while stationary, or reloading does not reset the budget. End a recovered travel leg after 24 campaign hours without such progress when safe; arriving at muster switches back to the normal gathering rules. Keep the checkpoint and original paid assembly deadline across save/load. This does not override native battle, movement, or player locks, a competing order, or normal strength, route, and eligibility checks.

**Retrying before camp:** A confirmed native strength failure records both the previous commitment and the actual force strength, not the scaled continuation probe. A fresh formation for the same kingdom and target needs materially weaker observed defenses or a quoted roster at least 10% (and at least 25 power) above the previous commitment. Weaker defenses mean a drop of at least 10% and 25 in observed interior strength, or lower walls or at least 25 less effective food without more interior strength. Even if weaker defenses waive the commitment floor, an early paid strength retry must quote enough power to exceed the prior failed force by that same material margin when the actual failed power is known. These floors apply to buying, not departure. After payment, retries obey the same 1.6× rule and their fixed assembly deadline.

An offensive attempt counts as paid from the first accepted positive summons debit, even if a later call fails. For two campaign days after its first pre-camp failure, allow at most one paid recovery for the same attacking kingdom and target owner, and require evidence that addresses the failure. For strength loss use the above rule; for a failed gathering order or stalled muster the proposed leader must already be at a legal muster and the necessary escorts must have land paths there and be due within 24 campaign hours. An unexplained loss has no early paid retry. If the paid recovery also fails before camp, stand down paid formations for 48 campaign hours from that closure, even if the second failure had a different cause. After stand-down, evaluate normally; retain any applicable strength guard. A recovery waiting to depart may take up to its live quoted assembly time plus 24 campaign hours and still obeys the normal no-progress rule; no timer authorizes an understrength march.

This record survives save/load and is not reset by a different leader, payer, muster or routine movement. Valid existing armies, truly free fresh formations, urgent relief and other targets are outside the target-level spending limit. Peace or relief diversion does not count as a failed attempt. Reaching the matching native camp or a change of target owner clears the episode; the seven-day repulse rule remains separate.

**Changing targets after a failed paid formation:** For eight campaign hours after a paid pre-camp strength or operational failure, do not buy a fresh siege roster at a different target if a strict majority of both the new paid escorts and any one failed attempt's paid escorts are the same parties. Count actual previous positive summons debits, including escorts that already left, not free calls, the leader, or the roster at dispersal. Check the chosen roster during search and before forming, and do not let price changes or refused calls bypass the limit at payment. Records survive reload. Different rosters, truly free plans, existing armies, and urgent relief stay eligible; same-target attempts keep their own recovery rules.

**Keeping or lifting an established siege:** Once camp is established, do not reapply the new-siege strength test. Waiting for a breach or a favorable native assault decision is expected. Reasons to lift include an unsustainable camp (e.g. actual starvation), the local relief threat defined below, or diversion to an uncovered higher-priority defense. A battle or assault already underway resolves normally; a commander decision cannot extract its participants.

**What counts as relief arriving:** On a siege review before battle, check the area **around the besieger camp** using the game's `SettlementDefendingNearbyPartyCheckRadius` (default land radius: `2.4 × GetEncounterJoiningRadius`, or 7.2 map units in the default model). Count only hostile field forces visible to the camp that can actually fight there; do not read enemy objectives, count a garrison behind the walls, or add attached parties twice. Compare their combined **field-battle strength** against that of the forces actually present in camp. If it is **strictly greater than 120%** of camp strength, lift the siege and clear the offensive order. This is the commander's withdrawal rule using a native distance, not vanilla besieger behavior. For a camp of 500 strength, 600 does not trigger withdrawal; 601 does if visible, local, and able to fight.

**After being pushed off:** When a local-threat withdrawal actually breaks the siege, or a relief battle defeat breaks it, record the campaign time for the **attacking kingdom and target fief**. For **7 full campaign days** that kingdom cannot select, muster for, or issue a new siege order against that fief, including as a neighboring follow-up or with a different army. It can still attack other fiefs and defend normally. A voluntary diversion for home or allied defense does not by itself start this timer. Save/load preserves the original expiry. At expiry the target becomes eligible for a fresh evaluation, not an automatic retry: it must pass the ordinary new-siege checks, and a known overwhelming local threat still rules it out. Do not treat a last sighting as live tracking.

**Commander offensive follow-up rule:** After capture or relief, consider an immediate offensive follow-up only at a neighbor of the completed objective's fortification on the game's fortification graph. Check the army's actual navigable route and strength before assigning it. No viable follow-up or defense means an eligible army enters idle grace. The one-ring limit applies to immediate follow-ons, not a later ordinary planning pass.

**Stay after a capture:** `StayHoursAfterSiege` in `_Module/Settings.xml` (default 0 when absent) delays the one-ring follow-up. The stay is counted from the observed capture, so native post-capture cleanup, which locks the leader's AI, uses part of it; the commander never overrides that lock. A commander army that captures a fortification and has no defense to take holds in place until capture + `StayHoursAfterSiege`, also while a home siege blocks offense. Relief and defense planners can still take it; offensive planners cannot. When the stay ends, check defense, then the one-ring follow-up once (skipping abandoned targets), then the ordinary 24-hour idle grace. The capture time, the stay and its end survive reload. Native dispersal, loss of authority and an unsustainable formation keep their own handling.

## Assigning the kingdom's forces

Consider existing AI armies, parties traveling to muster, and eligible independent parties together. Count each party once. Distinguish strength present now from strength that can arrive by the time it is needed.

- Compare existing armies alone and together, including those on lower-priority jobs. Record when the first response can arrive and when the combined force is expected to be able to fight. Each ready army can receive its defend order immediately; it need not wait at muster for a second army to depart.
- An army with an active home or allied relief assignment can be redirected intact, but its roster is unavailable for a replacement muster. This holds while gathering, marching, fleeing, or pursuing a party, even if the native defend order has temporarily cleared.
- If existing formations cannot do the job, evaluate a fresh muster from eligible free parties and parties that could be released from idle or lower-priority offensive armies. Count the time to break off, gather, and march from actual positions. Only break up armies if the replacement plus the remaining response makes an otherwise inadequate defense viable. This also applies on the tick a former assignment closes, before idle grace starts. An understrength response may use intact armies or muster free parties, but not disband a formation for its roster. Disbanding does not move troops across the map.
- Give higher-priority objectives first claim on the forces they **need**. Where more than one assignment can meet that need, prefer the one that also leaves other achievable objectives covered.

**Acceptance examples:** Either the eastern or western army can save our castle, but only the western army can reach an allied town in time. Send east to our castle and west to the ally. In a second scenario, both armies are conducting offensive sieges when a home town is besieged. Divert one if sufficient, both if they can relieve it together, or break up the necessary formations and muster a viable replacement if they cannot. The log must explain which response can arrive with enough strength and which existing sieges continue or are abandoned.

## Army lifecycle

1. **Identify the job.** Name the objective, target, and condition that ends the job. Being at war is not itself a reason to muster.
2. **Assign and gather.** Select a leader, eligible parties, and a muster point. Establish the strength needed to march and when it can be ready. Relief uses a 72-hour route and assembly estimate, not a prediction of when the siege will end. Track actual arrivals; a summons is not an arrival.
3. **Depart or cancel.** March when the gathered force can do the job. If missing parties, delay, or a changed target makes the job infeasible, reconsider rather than waiting indefinitely or marching understrength because a timer expired.
4. **Review the assignment.** If losses make an army too weak, reconsider the kingdom's forces for that objective. Do not routinely refill the same formation to its original size. Spend on cohesion only to sustain an assigned job. A new home siege can justify redirecting several armies at once.
5. **Close the job.** On success, failure, or loss of the target, disband each assigned army by default. Reuse it only for a specific nearby objective it can help complete in time with its current force. Make that decision separately for each army; two armies that relieved a town together need not stay together afterward.

### Forming an army: leader, muster, and departure

Start with the objective and the strength it would take to fight. For offense this is a minimum; for relief it is a target, not a condition for issuing the summons. The Stage 2 fixture below uses an absolute deadline; production siege relief instead uses the 72-hour remaining travel limit. In campaign, account for the armies that can reach the objective before deciding what still needs to muster.

1. **Where to gather:** Consider the kingdom's own towns and castles that are not under siege. Start with the one nearest the objective by reachable campaign route. If enough eligible parties cannot assemble there and reach the objective in time, try the next. Do not issue summons or spend influence merely to find out whether a muster is feasible.
2. **Who leads and who gets called:** At that muster point, try eligible AI leaders in order of travel time to the muster; break ties by stronger fighting party, then a stable party ID. Use the first whose clan can afford a viable summons. Count the leader once. Add eligible parties in arrival order, skipping those that would arrive too late or exceed the remaining call budget. Stop once enough strength is committed. Recheck the opt-out and availability before each call.
3. **When to march:** A commander siege uses its actual roster for the assignment and physically present strength for visible danger. For relief, if the desired strength cannot be assembled, depart with the available legal formation rather than waiting for troops that cannot be called. For a job with an explicit deadline, subtract remaining travel time to find the latest useful departure. Eligible roster members keep following the formation.
4. **When to cancel:** Cancel a particular muster if the objective disappears or the muster point is lost. An offensive muster can also fail its strength requirement; a job with an explicit deadline can run out of time. Being outmatched is not a cancellation reason for relief. A failed muster does not close an active relief objective: look for another route or force, without repeating the same failed summons on unchanged inputs.

**Stage 2 test fixture:** The job needs 300 fighting strength at the target by campaign hour 30. The march takes 8 hours, so the latest useful departure is hour 22. If 310 strength is present at hour 18, depart then, even if another party is on the way. In a second run, if only 240 is present and the first reinforcement that could close the gap cannot arrive until hour 24, cancel when that becomes known. These numbers are test inputs, not kingdom-wide balance settings. The Desktop log must show the required, summoned, and present strength separately, along with the deadline and actual departure or cancellation.

### Influence budget

Keep **200 influence per paying clan** available for kingdom votes. For all commander-ordered army spending, including initial summons, reinforcements, and cohesion boosts, the available budget is `max(0, current clan influence − 200)`. Check the combined cost when planning a muster and the clan's live balance before each payment. A free action remains eligible even below 200. The reserve does not restrict voting or the player's manual army orders.

When an assigned army's cohesion drops **below 50**, use the game's price to refill toward **100** without a random roll. If the full refill costs too much, buy the largest positive cohesion gain the clan can afford above its reserve. If none is affordable, skip the boost. Close a finished assignment before considering upkeep. Waiting under a native defend order is active service even if no reinforcement is known to be coming. Natural dispersal ends that army's assignment, not the kingdom's relief objective.

**Stages 2–3 acceptance checks:** At 250 influence, a summons costing 50 is affordable; one costing 51 is not. After paying 50, the clan cannot buy a positive-cost boost until it earns more influence. In a separate case, an assigned army is below 50 cohesion, its clan has 230 influence, and a full refill costs 45. Buy a partial refill at the native price for at most 30, and log the actual gain and remaining balance.

## Diagnostic logging

Use the Desktop log file as a readable campaign timeline. Identify campaign time, kingdom, objective, army, and relevant parties consistently enough to follow the same operation across save/load. Log decisions when made or materially changed, rather than repeating an unchanged conclusion every tick.

| When | What the log must explain |
| --- | --- |
| A decision is made, declined, or blocked | Trigger, relevant eligibility or priority, decisive inputs, chosen action or reason for taking none. For a contested assignment, show why the alternative lost. For a blocked vanilla action, distinguish the attempted decision from any actual state change. |
| A summons or movement order is issued | Parties selected or excluded and why, target, expected strength and timing. Record arrivals, cancellations, departure strength, and whether the army reached or abandoned the target. |
| Native execution of a commander order | Distinguish defend order, arrival, observed waiting/regrouping, engagement, and siege outcome. For offense, distinguish besiege order, camp start or join, and assault. Record transitions and relevant observed strengths without issuing extra orders just to produce a log entry. |
| Besiegers are pushed off | Record the attacking kingdom, target, camp strength, observed enemy forces counted, distances, radius, and resulting strength ratio (or actual relief battle defeat). Distinguish the order to lift from the siege ending. Log the seven-day expiry and the reason a retry was rejected or permitted. |
| Army spending is considered | Record the paying clan, current influence, budget above the reserve, cost, and whether the action was taken or skipped. For a payment, record actual influence and any cohesion change. |
| An operation ends or is interrupted | Distinguish a commander-ordered disbandment from a natural game outcome. Record the cause, affected parties, and, for cohesion loss, the last known cohesion and boost decision, if any. Keep enough state to identify what resumed after loading. |

## Build stages and campaign acceptance tests

Pass each stage in campaign **and** verify that its log explains the result before building the next. Run later stages against the earlier authority and player-protection rules as well.

### Unattended campaign acceptance

For an acceptance run, the coordinator supplies a test build or arms the next test run before launch. The tester loads a disposable campaign save and allows campaign time to advance. The mod starts the pending scenario when the campaign map is ready: no console sequence, target-ID lookup, status polling, or intermediate acknowledgments. Ordinary play does not start the fixture.

The runner uses the current campaign to choose feasible participants and objectives, performs the same actions the commander uses, and checks actual outcomes. For the outstanding army-reuse test, success means the same army completed its guard, received the neighboring siege assignment, and reached the target to start or join a native camp. A positive preview or an issued order alone is not a pass. If the world changes, the runner can try another candidate within a bounded time and attempt budget. Distinguish a failed assertion from a scenario the campaign could not exercise. Do not manufacture a war, change ownership, or spawn troops to make a test turn green.

For this reuse-only scenario, muster and guard can be the same frontier fort. Require native settlement presence and ready physical strength before issuing the guard. Budget gathering and siege travel against the remaining run with movement headroom; do not add a separate friendly march to the acceptance path or change the manual march deadline rules.

End with one in-game popup and a matching summary with a run ID in `Desktop/BannerlordMilitaryAIOverhaul.log`: what passed, failed, or could not be tested, and why. A completed run must not restart on reload. Headless checks continue to cover deterministic edge cases; the player is not expected to reproduce every fixture by hand.

**Native order checks:** In Stage 4, send 400 strength against 600 besiegers on land: verify departure, approach, and native waiting without a mod-issued hold. Bring a second responding force close enough to tip the local odds; verify native reassessment and record whether an engagement starts. In Stage 6, verify no camp exists at departure, the camp starts or is joined on arrival, and a prepared camp can launch a native assault without a separate commander order.

**Stage 6 retry check:** Move an overwhelming hostile field force from outside the local radius to visible contact inside it. Verify the camp withdraws once, but cannot escape a battle already underway. After the siege breaks, move the enemy away and try the same target with a different army: the kingdom must still reject it on day six, including after reload. After seven full days, test once with a known overwhelming threat still there and once when the threat has gone. Only the latter may proceed if the ordinary siege checks also pass.

### Stage 0 — takeover of an existing campaign

On first takeover, release existing vanilla AI-led armies in every kingdom. Disband the **army**, not its individual parties. Release parties still on the way to muster as well as those already attached. Leave player-led armies alone.

If releasing an army would pull participants out of an active battle or siege, wait until that event ends. Let the event resolve normally; do not let vanilla select a new objective in the meantime. If the army is destroyed during the event, record that outcome instead of attempting a second release. This is a one-time takeover, not a purge on every save load.

**Campaign checks:** An idle or marching AI army releases its parties; an engaged army stays in its event and is released afterward if it survives. Save and reload with a release pending: the Desktop log must show why it waited and how it ended, exactly once. Before the mod gains its own creation logic in Stage 2, zero new AI-led armies is the expected result; it is not sufficient proof by itself. Also verify a blocked vanilla formation attempt, continuing independent party orders, and successful player army management.

## Deferred scope

**Deferred:** Preventive defense before a siege begins. Revisit scouting, report delays, and stale sightings only if that feature is taken up. It is not a prerequisite for siege relief.
