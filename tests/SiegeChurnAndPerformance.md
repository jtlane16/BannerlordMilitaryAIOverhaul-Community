# Offensive muster churn and planning work

## Current safeguards

After an assignment really closes, match `Army idle grace started` and `Army idle grace ended` by army ID. The `until` is fixed at start + 24 campaign hours. Match reassignment to the same army's siege/relief order and actual debit rows; an idle start is not a dispersal. At the deadline expect either an intact assignment or `Muster release` with `reason=idle-grace-expired` when unlocked. Compare against `81cc27a7`'s 17.1-hour gap between stand-down and fresh Lageta purchase; no specific saving is guaranteed.

The earlier audit below describes the rule in place at that time. Fresh offensive formations now test the minimum affordable roster against the native score at 90% of projected strength. A separate save-persistent guard records confirmed native strength failures before camp by kingdom and target. Neither a cosmetic change to the roster nor a different muster site is enough to spend again on the same objective; the candidate needs materially more power or observable weaker defenses, and a passing live quote. Established camps, existing formations and relief do not use this added purchase gate.

The current lifecycle separates purchases, assignments and disbands. Check the live native roster at 1.6× adjusted defense from formation through march, including zero-cost members. Only present strength is available to fight. Prefer intact formations before buying; after a confirmed assignment failure, consider a safe regroup or intact reassignment before releasing. Compare equal campaign hours from the same starting save. Match `Muster debit`, `Siege departure` (`roster`, `present`, `checkedPower`), `Siege regroup`, `Siege reassigned intact`, `Siege stand-down`, native camp and capture by **army ID**. An assignment that closed but whose army got another job is not a wasted formation. Count repeated payments, relief diversions, unresolved armies and formations stranded without progress separately.

`TestHistoricalLifecycleReplay` exercises the production decision paths using rounded strengths from `b3f0dbcf` (Jalmarys 1,701 present, 2,549 roster), `7a9164b6` (Amitatys 2,209 to 1,532 after three parties left), and `531989d3` (Vladiv 1,024 present with travelers). It supplies score pass/fail bands and camp/capture events, not simulated combat outcomes or exact historical native scores. The same suite covers field regroup, reassignment, reload, legitimate stand-down and stalled march. A live campaign comparison remains the release validation for frequency of churn.

Paid failures before camp have a separate kingdom/target/owner episode. Search and commit apply the same admission policy, and the debit hook prevents a free quote from turning into a positive charge. An evidence-backed retry that also fails starts a 48-campaign-hour paid stand-down; unknown first failures also wait 48 hours. The failed-strength retry floor applies to the retry purchase only. See `SPEC.md` for the precise rules.

For a live check against `0427dc2f`, use `Siege paid attempt` and `Siege paid pre-camp failure` (match `attempt`; `recovery`, `paid`, `policy`, `reviewAfter`), `Muster debit` (match army ID), and `Siege selected` (`paidRetryFloor`, `commitPower`). Verify that no third paid attempt begins during its stand-down, purchase floors are met, and camps or ownership changes reset the episode. Count first-failure and repeat-failure influence separately, alongside camps, time to camp, assignments still active, and urgent relief. Fixture passes demonstrate the rule; only campaign replays can measure how much it reduces churn.

- **A real gathering loss, followed by avoidable immediate repurchase.** At `2214728.28`, EW5 projection fell from `2026.51` to `1549.60` with two fewer travelers. `CharacterObject_2748_party_1` and `CharacterObject_2749_party_1` were previously paid Sturgian travelers (combined roughly 484 power at `2214704.02`), and the `2214728.82` report places both in Southern Empire. Closing EW5 was justified. The next search in the *same pass* assigned the same leader to S5, summoning four survivors from the old release list for **32 of the replacement's 67 influence**. The existing failed-muster cache only remembers the leader/muster/target combination and a free-pool fingerprint, so a new target/site bypassed it. Repeated S5 musters cost **224** in total, but used three different formation IDs and changing force pools. There is no proof the latter two were the same viable alternatives with unchanged inputs.
- **A positive native score does not measure strength margin.** In installed `DefaultTargetScoreCalculatingModel.GetTargetScoreForFaction`, the fresh-siege zero gate compares supplied strength against twice adjusted defense. Wall/food, garrison, militia, defenders inside and inferred defending kingdom support matter. The hero's apparent `RandomFloat(0.1f)` reads the hero's saved random value, not a new roll per query. Nearby threats, approach-dependent distance, value, relations and existing target affect the subsequent score; they are not an extractable threshold or headroom. The scorer is the same actual leader at selection, gathering and departure, not a hypothetical party at the muster site. Before formation it receives projected independent strength; during gathering it receives allowed attached strength plus actual eligible escorts; departure and march use present strength. Summons can change movement, attachments and ETA; they do not teleport or make escorts present. The two EW1 departures had already released paid travelers (14 and 16 influence respectively) because actual strength passed then. The old log lacks defense inputs and precise failing strength to prove why later checks failed. New `Siege feasibility lost` lines separate model failure from observed threat and log actual/projected power and scorer context; selection logs the chosen vs full affordable arrival-order prefix, not a global strongest affordable roster or reconstructed gate margin.
- **Changes:** parties released on a failed offense feasibility check cannot be bought back by offense in that same pass. Other forces may still be selected, defense can still respond, and the next ordinary search can use real newly available reinforcement. This is not a cap, a target cooldown, or a change to the seven-day repulse rule. No extra paid reserve or arbitrary strength buffer was added: the spec says to stop at an affordable viable force, and the log cannot establish how much spare strength, if any, would have prevented each later cancellation. Established camps continue to bypass the new-siege check.

**When offense is tried again:** the exclusion matches the exact campaign hour of the failed formation's release. It is gone at the next different campaign timestamp, but that does not trigger a search. The normal scheduler visits one eligible kingdom per search slot (slots are at least 0.25 campaign hours apart) and does not revisit a kingdom until at least 8 campaign hours after its last visit. If that kingdom was searched in the release pass, its earliest next visit is therefore release hour + 8, potentially later if other kingdoms get the slot first. If it was not searched, its existing due time is unchanged. For a recorded failed pre-departure muster, the same leader/muster/target is still skipped while that entry and its free-force fingerprint are unchanged; that cache is not a permanent target ban. A different tuple, a changed fingerprint, or a failed march not recorded in that cache can be reconsidered on an ordinary visit and still must pass live checks. Only an actual repulse can start the separate seven-day rule.

## Stutter audit and deterministic benchmarks

The session contains 6,821 rows over about 112 wall seconds / 15 accelerated campaign days. It includes 3,951 `Force party` rows and peaks at 373 printed rows in one second. It does **not** measure render frame time, profiler samples, or even individual file writes.

- **Shared production relief:** callbacks and periodic reviews can rescan the world. Previously each responding kingdom enumerated its own and allied fiefs, including non-sieges. Now the review builds the live besieged-fief list once and still revalidates owner, camp and diplomacy for each responding kingdom. Event triggers, priority and dispatch are unchanged. Coverage/force inventories still refresh after mutations, where using stale membership would change the answer.
- **Shared production offense:** staggered kingdom search and active review are separate. Route distance is still necessary for each origin/target and never replaced by straight-line ranking. Identical native scores at alternative muster sites were being recalculated even though the scorer saw the same actual leader, target and strength. The new memo exists only within one read-only search. Assembly, dispatch, and march still use live scores. Departure no longer runs the same gate again with no intervening changes; if an escort was released, it rechecks. Active muster routes and quarter-hour reviews, coverage recalculation, threat observations and native campaign callbacks remain possible costs.
- **Observer:** ordinary builds exclude it. Coordination mode checks actual commitments at quarter-hour intervals and captures candidate routes only while armed at relevant production choices. Completed runs stop. The other opt-in modes have their own search/watch behavior. The profiling fixture does not attribute frame stalls to the observer.
- **Inventory and logging:** the full inventory builds estimated strengths and formats per-party diagnostic lines on a daily refresh, not every frame. `CampaignLog.Events` batches a report into one append, although it still formats its individual rows. Other events may append individually on the campaign thread. Neither logging volume nor this fixture demonstrates disk latency. No report rows, notifications, or lifecycle evidence were suppressed.

Run `MAIO_BENCHMARK=1` in the environment with `tests/RunSmoke.ps1`. The fixtures warm up before five stopwatch samples; baseline and revised runs use the same world inputs and candidate rules. They stub native scene distance and the target score, and capture logs in memory. The benchmark counts caller-side modeled land distance calls, not all native path work inside the real score function. It does not measure game FPS, actual filesystem latency, or the whole campaign simulation.
