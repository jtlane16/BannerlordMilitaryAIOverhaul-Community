## Native mechanics and decision

`KingdomCommander.SiegeScore` calls `GetTargetScoreForFaction(target, Army.ArmyTypes.Besieger, actualLeaderParty, suppliedPower)`. Inspect the installed `DefaultTargetScoreCalculatingModel` (`TaleWorlds.CampaignSystem.cs` 320891–321149), the native Army constructor (`20414`), `MobileParty.Army` setter (`535667`), `SetPartyAiAction.GetActionForVisitingSettlement` (`18303`), and call pricing (`297398`). The explicit mission argument is **Besieger even though the newly constructed native army starts as Defender**. The stock fresh-offense branch does not substitute the actual army's number of men, `ArmyType`, a hypothetical muster position, or `DefaultBehavior` for `suppliedPower`.

- Search uses actual pre-formation leader state and the candidate's affordable projected prefix; the new commit repeats the same explicit native call with live estimates and state **before** mutation. Native creation changes membership; `Visit` changes target/order. Calls give escort orders and do not teleport. Gather uses the allowed present + eligible reachable travelers (using the same estimated-strength context), departure requires **attached present**, march uses **attached present**, and an established camp is not a fresh siege. The actual scorer's navigation context and target multiplier can change; the latter alters utility, not the fresh gate. A stored hero `RandomValue` supplies the hero term, not a new random draw each time. Source review found no automatic fresh gate penalty simply for being the leader of an army.
- Stock fresh gate: compare supplied power to `2 × (weighted occupant estimated power × (food-adjusted walls − hero/aggression term) + potential defending-faction support + 0.1)`. Weighted occupants include garrison, militia, and eligible visitors, with special player weights. Faction support uses current target faction strength and settlement count, and attacker map-event/camp context matters in the relevant branches. Nearby defender distance, target commitment, utility/value, diplomacy/relations, etc. also feed the eventual score. `28.7 → 0` does not give a 28.7-point margin or identify a single input. A positive score at power X cannot guarantee a pass at power X a campaign hour later. New selection and failure diagnostics include wall, food, raw occupant power buckets, faction strength, aggression and scorer target; they are context, not a reimplementation of the score.
- Twelve of the 18 gathering failures logged *at least* original quoted power within 0.05 (not proof individual rosters were unchanged), and 14 had more affordable arrival-prefix strength at selection. For castle EW2 the actual fresh threshold appears to have moved even during multiple successful assemblies; the old records lack defender inputs to say why. For other cases forces legitimately shrank or their route/ownership/political state changed.

**Remedy chosen:** one scheduled confirmation of a failed projected native check, only while the leader is still en route to the muster, the roster has an escort and the same eligible projected identities, power hasn't dropped by more than 0.05, and there is no known overwhelming threat or existing no-progress expiry. Confirmation is due at first failure +0.25 campaign hours; an event-triggered review earlier can observe recovery or an immediate hard blocker but cannot turn that same failed signal into a second scheduled failure. No new call, upkeep, offensive order or spare strength purchase while waiting; recovery retains the exact formation, and a sustained failure closes. Genuine loss, threat, lost route, blocked objective, failed actual departure and failed march retain their protections and gates. This specifically addresses avoidable one-sample releases. It is **not** a claim that EW1 would have recovered, or a fix for all 26 failures.

Alternatives considered:

- **Buy a robust prefix.** Some attempts had headroom available. It costs more and holds more parties away from defense, often for a longer ETA. The full affordable prefix is not a future threshold; four of these gathering failures show no spare prefix at selection. Adding a fixed percentage or an extra escort doesn't follow from the recorded inputs and may only transfer cost into otherwise healthy formations. No policy change made.
- **Keep or retarget after persistent failure.** A short unchanged gathering confirmation is safe because it gives *no* new siege order. Keeping a failed march order could let it enter a camp before another commander check. Retargeting a persistently invalid army requires a new current route/priority/strength decision, and there is no evidence the alternate was viable at the original time from the assembled force. Don't park armies indefinitely or issue a march while unviable. Prompt relief can still use a lawful diversion; sustainable established camps never go through fresh strength.
- **Persist unchanged no-op scans.** A negative result cannot be inferred from the free roster alone. Occupants, ownership, prices, garrisons, food, visible threats, party position and live native calculations change. Keep only sweep-local memoization and the existing narrow exact failed-muster tuple/fingerprint; do not freeze native feasibility across ordinary visits. Eliminate avoidable negative-candidate scratch paths and distribute the remaining repeated visits. This addresses the measured pacing issue without a new cap, longer campaign-time retry policy, or changes to war/defense.

## Search scheduling, ordering and measurement

Main world offense only queues eligibility on campaign ticks (including catch-up). The mod's real `OnApplicationTick` invokes at most one outstanding sweep slice or one commit, only on a running campaign map. Relief and siege lifecycle reviews still run on their original campaign path. Commit also waits for any pending relief review. Oldest due kingdom first, stable native-order tie; existing 0.25 campaign-hour search slot and eight campaign-hour kingdom revisit eligibility still come from actual search start. A long sweep can delay the next visit, not advance it. There is no hard concurrent-formation cap.

The sweep evaluates the same ranked set, with native route lengths for ranking, the same value/existing-force/id tiebreaks, first viable leader in each muster arrival order, and affordable viable prefix. Straight distance is only the same pruning lower bound. It does not take the first result merely because a frame ended. Ships/nav and other eligibility checks still run. One active, non-mutating sweep at a time. Retain sampled quotes only in that sweep; do not pretend all quotes are an atomic snapshot of a moving campaign. A newly competitive legal target/origin, displaced scored ready army, or invalid chosen order can discard a proposal; a failed commit buys nothing and the next normal visit recomputes. A movement/price/strength change elsewhere can change what would win a hypothetical simultaneous fresh scan; an exhaustive re-run at commit would reintroduce the measured stall. No claim of simultaneous optimality across moving samples.

The scheduled commit runs separately, revalidates the chosen target, authority, current defense reservations, live route/visit/summon eligibility, current power, affordable reserve/price and nearby threats. The native debit hook is still final after mutation. The exact-hour release exclusion applies when picking candidates **and** on the live commit hour, and still doesn't trigger a search or apply to defense or later ordinary visits. Native event callbacks may still change an army *during* commit; an actual failed join is handled live, not assumed paid/projected.

Each exhaustive slice stops at 8 modeled quote/route/path units, 64 scheduler steps, or a soft 2 ms elapsed budget, whichever comes first. `GetSettlementValueForFaction`, the scorer, and native price are units because they themselves can call native distance models. One native call, snapshot/pair sorting, and a spatial enumeration cannot be preempted inside a callback. Contact paths are individually sliced; the native spatial enumeration isn't held across frames. The separate commit has one roster-length validation pass plus native construction/summons; the short one-ring capture follow-on remains on review and is not the world search. So this is a per-callback **aggregate search bound**, not a hard wall-time limit for the game. Use the same `siegePlan` field to compare both builds; the new `siegeCommitSubset` labels the nested commit portion without changing the capture format, arming or two-minute limit.

### Headless verification

`MAIO_BENCHMARK=1` with `RunSmoke.ps1` compares the same worker run monolithically against the production callback scheduler in a warmed no-change world: 14 possible leaders, eight muster origins, seven objectives, 12 repeated no-order visits for each failure mode after two warmups. The deterministic stub charges 220 µs per score, 100 µs per explicit distance, 135 µs per scratch path trial, 35 µs per price and 45 µs per native value quote. The modeled totals per visit are identical between the executors, with no cross-visit caching; strength-rejected visits avoid all 784 possible scratch trials while path-rejected visits still exercise them. A separate run injects one 12 ms distance query to show it sets a lower bound for that callback. Results and DLL hash are in the staged transcripts. These are injected workload costs, not real native or GPU measurements. Elapsed aggregate work can stay similar when slice tails fall. Callback count, time to complete a scan under campaign acceleration, discard frequency and actual pacing should be compared in normal play only after approval.
